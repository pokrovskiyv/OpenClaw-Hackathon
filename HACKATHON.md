## The Challenge

Build a **multi-agent claims processing system** for a fictional car insurance company: **Ohio Mutual Auto**.

Ohio Mutual Auto's claims process is entirely manual — phone calls, paper forms, blurry photos texted to an agent. Each claim passes through six people before it gets resolved.

---

## The Pipeline

| # | Role | Responsibility |
|---|------|---------------|
| 1 | **Front Desk** | Registers and categorizes the claim |
| 2 | **Claims Officer** | Checks whether coverage applies |
| 3 | **Assessor** | Estimates the actual damage |
| 4 | **Fraud Analyst** | Looks for suspicious patterns |
| 5 | **Senior Reviewer** | Makes the final decision |
| 6 | **Finance** | Executes payment |

> **REGULATIONS EXIST AND YOU HAVE TO RESPECT THEM. BUSINESS PRIORITIES EXIST AND YOU HAVE TO RESPECT THEM, TOO. DISREGARD EITHER AND YOU'RE OUT OF BUSINESS (AND NOT WINNING THE HACKATHON).**

---

## Secret Addition

On the day, teams receive an additional piece of the challenge — business context that shapes and complicates design decisions. Plan for human reasoning; your thinking should not be hardcoded.

---

## Required Tooling

**OpenClaw** (aka Moltbot, Clawdbot) is mandatory. Each role in the pipeline = an OpenClaw agent, appropriately scoped. You may use anything else in addition, but must demonstrate meaningful use of OpenClaw in your system.

---

## Judging Criteria

10 minutes per team: 5-min presentation + 5-min Q&A.

| Criterion | Weight |
|-----------|--------|
| **Business Thinking** — Can you defend decisions from a business perspective? | 50% |
| **System Thinking** — Does it work end-to-end? Does the multi-agent design make sense? | 50% |
