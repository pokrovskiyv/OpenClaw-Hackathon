# Practical Car Accident Checklist in the U.S.

Below is a practical step-by-step guide for what to do after a car accident in the U.S. (rules vary somewhat by state).

## 1) Right After the Accident — Safety and 911

- **Stop immediately** and **do not leave the scene** (even if the damage looks minor).
- Check whether anyone is injured. If there are injuries or any doubt, **call 911**.
- If the car is drivable and it is safe to do so, move it out of the traffic lane and turn on your hazard lights.
- Do not argue at the scene and do not admit fault (let the police and insurance determine responsibility).

## 2) Exchange Information (Required)

At the scene, exchange the following with the other driver:

- Full name and contact information
- Driver’s license number
- Vehicle registration
- Insurance company name + policy number
- Vehicle details (make / model / year / VIN / license plate)

In many states, this is a legal obligation. For example, the **NY DMV** specifically requires drivers to stop and exchange information.

## 3) Call the Police and Get Report Details

Even in a “minor” accident, it is often better to call the police (or at least ask how to file a **self-report** if officers do not respond to the scene).

Important information to get from the police:

- Officer’s name
- **Badge number**
- Report number / report ID
- Where and when you can get a copy of the report

This helps a lot when the insurance company reviews the claim.

## 4) Document Everything at the Scene (Evidence)

If it is safe, take photos/videos of:

- All vehicles (from multiple angles)
- License plates
- Vehicle damage
- Accident location / road / lane markings
- Skid marks and road conditions
- A wide shot of the overall scene

Also write down:

- Time and exact location
- Weather conditions
- Witness contact information
